## `sync_mode` comparison

One additional 5-second request used `sync_mode: true`:

| Delivery | API wall | Post-response decode/write | Total | Response size | Video size |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CDN URL (`sync_mode: false`) | 2.601 s | 1.387 s download | 3.988 s | small JSON | 4.22 MB |
| Inline base64 (`sync_mode: true`) | 6.370 s | 0.003 s | 6.374 s | 5.75 MB | 4.31 MB |

In this sample, inline base64 was slower than receiving and fully downloading the CDN video. Base64 expanded the response by roughly one third. The browser can avoid writing the inline result to disk, but it must still receive and decode the larger response.

## Visual findings

- Each individual clip maintained its characters and scene well across its own duration.
- Independent text-to-video generations did not preserve exact cross-clip identity. The fox remained recognizable, but the robot's body, face, proportions, and forest design changed materially between runs.
- Prompt repetition alone is therefore insufficient for seamless story continuity.

## Realtime implication

- Five-second clips leave too little safety margin for a production realtime loop: only about 1.2 seconds remained after a full download in this run, and the inline-base64 run was slower than playback.
- Ten-second clips provide a healthier buffer: approximately 4.4 seconds between full local availability and the end of the current clip.
- Fifteen-second clips provide the most buffer but commit the story to a long visual action and reduce steering responsiveness.
- Product default: one 5-second opening followed by continuous 10-second image-conditioned clips until the child steers. The rolling queue is bounded to two clips. New guidance cancels unseen work, lets the visible clip finish, and branches the next 10-second clip from its final frame.
- Use the prior clip's final frame as the next image-to-video input to preserve the visual seam. H3 Max's official image-to-video endpoint accepts a first frame and an optional end frame; its latency must be benchmarked separately before implementation.

## Conditioned-generation comparison

Two additional 10-second, 480p runs used `prompt_expansion_mode: disabled`, `sync_mode: false`, and small JPEG references:

| Endpoint | References | Provider inference | API wall | Download | Total to local bytes | Encoded duration | Realtime result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Image-to-video | One 62.6 KB prior final frame | 1.881 s | 3.766 s | 1.835 s | 5.601 s | 10.144 s | 1.81× faster than playback |
| Reference-to-video | Two 512×512 character crops, 58.7 KB total | 4.057 s | 13.547 s | 1.433 s | 14.980 s | 10.144 s | Slower than playback |

Visual assessment:

- Image-to-video reproduced the previous clip's final composition as its opening frame, then preserved the same fox, robot, palette, and environment through a continuous move into the next location. This is the best live scene-to-scene path tested.
- Reference-to-video preserved the two independently supplied character designs very well in a new composition. It was substantially slower and therefore does not fit the critical live branch path at the measured latency.
- Reference-to-video remains useful before playback begins: establish canonical characters, generate an opening/idle asset, or prepare reusable character-consistent scenes. Live branch continuation should use the prior final frame with image-to-video.

Recommended hybrid:

1. Use reference-to-video outside the realtime path to establish or validate canonical character identity.
2. Use image-to-video for each live story Beat, conditioning on the previous Beat's final frame.
3. Keep a 10-second clip visible while the next 10-second image-conditioned clip generates and preloads. The measured conditioned run leaves enough buffer while improving steering responsiveness over 15-second clips.

## Three-Beat prompt-only guided-story prototype

The prototype reused the earlier prompt-generated opening and first image-conditioned continuation, then generated a second image-conditioned continuation from Beat 2's final frame. No manually designed character sheet or external visual reference was used; all continuity descended from the opening text prompt.

Fake selected path:

1. Fox and robot cross the moonlit stream.
2. They discover a meadow of golden fireflies.
3. The child chooses “Follow the golden fireflies,” leading them to a tiny glowing door.

| Beat | Mode | API wall | Download | Total to bytes | Encoded duration |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Text-to-video, balanced expansion | 4.007 s | 1.714 s | 5.721 s | 10.144 s |
| 2 | Image-to-video, disabled expansion | 3.766 s | 1.835 s | 5.601 s | 10.144 s |
| 3 | Image-to-video, disabled expansion | 3.782 s | 1.646 s | 5.428 s | 10.144 s |

Combined output: 30.432 seconds. Sequential generation and full downloads totaled 16.750 seconds, or about 1.82 seconds of finished video per wall-clock second.

With choices displayed at 3 seconds and selected at 4 seconds:

- Beat 2 is fully available at 9.601 seconds, leaving 0.543 seconds before the current clip ends.
- Beat 3 is fully available at 9.428 seconds, leaving 0.716 seconds before the current clip ends.
- A late selection at 8 seconds requires roughly 3.3–3.5 seconds of final-frame hold time.

Visual assessment: both characters remained recognizable through two consecutive image-to-video generations. The robot's face panel, moss placement, body proportions, fox scarf, moonlit palette, and illustration language remained stable enough to read as one continuous story. The second continuation also followed the requested child choice and ended on the requested stable composition.

## Complex-action stress test

Two more 10-second image-conditioned Beats continued from the prompt-only chain, deliberately replacing simple travel with coordinated, high-motion actions.

Beat 4 requested a cooperative mechanism puzzle: fireflies reveal controls, the fox jumps and hangs from a vine lever, the robot braces and turns a wheel, the door opens, and the friends high-five. Beat 5 requested a rescue climax: a star-seed flies away, the fox launches after it, the robot catches the fox, the seed activates the tree, and the tree blooms.

| Beat | API wall | Download | Total to bytes | Encoded duration | Full-download headroom after a choice at 4s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 — cooperative puzzle | 3.513 s | 1.577 s | 5.090 s | 10.144 s | 1.054 s |
| 5 — rescue climax | 3.936 s | 1.681 s | 5.616 s | 10.144 s | 0.528 s |

Observed action adherence:

- Beat 4 clearly showed the fox using the hanging vine, the robot interacting with the wheel, the mechanism opening the glowing door, and the final celebratory gesture.
- Beat 5 clearly showed the fox launching toward the glowing star-seed, the robot catching/supporting the fox, the magical transfer into the tree, the bloom, and a stable ending tableau.
- Higher motion introduced normal pose variation but did not break the characters' recognizable identity or the scene-to-scene visual seam.

The full five-Beat chain is 50.720 seconds. The final two action clips required 10.706 seconds combined to generate and fully download, while providing 20.288 seconds of playback.
